Building JrKCCurrent with My Daughter Madison

Technology October 11, 2025

This has been one of my favorite projects. My 4th grade daughter, Madison, and I built jrkccurrent.com together. It started as a simple idea: let's make a website that shows the water level and flow of our favorite creeks and rivers. Madison is a budding scientist and loves to check the data before we go kayaking.

The Stat Tracker: Our Data Detective

The heart of our project is the 'stat_tracker'. Think of it as our data detective. It goes out to the internet and finds the latest information from the US Geological Survey (USGS). The USGS has sensors in rivers all over the country that measure how high the water is and how fast it's moving. Our stat tracker grabs this data for the spots we care about, like the Kansas River.

Madison helped me pick which locations to track. She would say, "Dad, let's add the Wakarusa River!" and we would look up the special code for that USGS sensor. The stat tracker runs on a schedule, so it's always up-to-date.

The Stats Page: Where the Numbers Tell a Story

Once our data detective has the information, we need a place to see it. That's where the 'stats' page comes in. This is the page Madison and I are most proud of. It's a big, colorful chart that shows the water level over the last few days. Madison had a great idea to use different colors to show if the water is high or low. Green means it's a good level for kayaking, yellow means it's a little high, and red means it's too high and might be dangerous.

We spent a lot of time making the chart easy to read. Madison would tell me, "The lines are too skinny," or "Can we make the numbers bigger?". It was a great way for her to see how we can take boring numbers and turn them into a fun and useful picture.

What We Learned

Building this website together has been a blast. Madison has learned a lot about how websites work, where data comes from, and how to make it useful. I've learned that building something with your kids is one of the most rewarding things you can do. It's not just about the code; it's about the time spent together, solving problems and creating something new.
